# Probability for Stats

## Why Probability Deserves More Attention
> When we use a statistical model, what assumptions are we actually making?

Probability is often treated as a side topic — something to race through before getting to "real" statistics. But in truth, probability is the foundation of statistics. Every time we fit a model, report a confidence interval, or talk about uncertainty, we are implicitly relying on probability theory.

This course is an invitation to take probability seriously — not just as a set of rules to memorize, but as a language for expressing uncertainty, reasoning under incomplete information, and making decisions in the presence of randomness.

### What You’ll Learn

We’ll start from the basics:

- How to count outcomes systematically
- How to understand events and sample spaces
- How to define and calculate probabilities

Then we’ll build up to:

- Conditional probability and independence
- Bayes’ Theorem — how to update beliefs

You’ll learn both the mathematics and the intuition behind these ideas, with concrete examples and a fun game to test your knowledge at the end. 
There are plenty of exercises to help you practice, and you are encouraged to work through them. However, all of the exercises have solutions available, so you can check your work.

### Why It Matters
Whether you're testing a hypothesis, building a regression model, or just interpreting a graph, you are always — implicitly — doing probability.

This course is meant to give you the tools to:

- Think clearly about uncertainty
- Understand what your models are really assuming
- Recognize when probabilities are being misused
